The curly-kumo tag

One line of HTML places a cloud and animates its growth. On the tag, growth runs from 0 to 100. The three examples are live.

Fed by JavaScript

        
The page sends a number from 0 to 100 and the cloud follows it. Here the number is collected from pointer movement.

        

        

        

        

      

      

        
Scroll

        
Scroll inside the box.

        

          
The first cloud uses enter and leave. It grows in when half of it is visible and grows out when less than half is visible.

          

          
          
The second cloud uses scroll-link. Its growth follows the scroll position. It is 0 when its centre is at the bottom edge of the box and 100 when its centre reaches the middle. Stop scrolling and it stops.

How a cloud is built

      
    
        
  1. A cloud is one to five lobed masses that overlap. Later masses sit in front of earlier ones.
    2. 
        
  2. A mass is a ring of round lobes of unequal size. Its outline is one closed path.
    3. 
        
  3. A mass is kept thin, so the curls from its edge reach its middle.
    4. 
        
  4. A curl starts at a cusp of its mass outline and winds into its own lobe. Where two curls would cross, the older one is hidden behind the younger lobe.
    5. 
        
  5. As growth rises, the first mass appears and gains lobes at two fixed points. Further masses then emerge from its edge, starting at zero size.
    6. 
      

    

    

      
What the generator returns

      
    
        
  1. Geometry only: for each mass, one outline path and its curl paths, listed back to front.
    2. 
        
  2. The silhouette of the whole cloud as one path, and the bounding box.
    3. 
        
  3. No stroke width, colour or fill margin. The page applies the preview style on top.
    4. 
        
  4. The same settings always return the same paths.
